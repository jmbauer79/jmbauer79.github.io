    I made today another day without a an albergue reservation. I left later
    starting off on my own again, around 7:30 am, leaving after about half the
    group and before the rest. 


    A 15 mile walk is starting to feel normal. I’m getting into a rhythm. I’ll walk for myself for a while, and then have a conversation with a fellow pilgrim, either someone I know (now that it’s been more than a few days, we’re starting to recognize each other) or someone new if we collectively seem open to chat. You can usually tell. 


    It was a pensive day. On the one hand, it was another gorgeous day.

    It was a nice balance of reflection and conversation, but honestly coming off the high from [last night](https://jbwndrs.com/wanders/camino-de-santiago/2026-09-09-no-reservation/) was rough today. I missed being around everyone, even though I still had some meaningful conversations along the way. 


    I stopped in a church. They had left some pre-printed prayers for pilgrims. I’ll leave somethings unwritten here but will just say it felt like the words on that piece of paper were speaking directly to me. 


    I continued the walk to today’s summit, at about the halfway point, in conversation with Erzebet to encounter the we’ll know Monumento al Peregrino art installation. The series of sillouettes represent the same walk pilgrims have made to Santiago for many centuries.

content_block_5:
  narrative: >-
    I was there not five minutes and I ran into Odetta, a pilgrim from Italy who
    was at last night’s special dinner. What serendipity! We chatted for a few
    and I continued walking across the bridge. Then I saw Leslie from Sacramento
    and Sophie? (I can’t recall her name) from Paris - both also at last night’s
    dinner. Then the three of us ran into Scott, one more from the same dinner,
    all at the bridge around the same time. It’s exactly what I needed. 


    I ended up spending the next couple hours with Leslie and Sophie, the latter of whom speaks no English or Spanish, so it was a great opportunity to put my very limited French to use. 


    Not long after, I saw a message in a pilgrims WhatsApp group about dinner plans that evening. I was all in.


    I’m back now, getting ready for bed, heart full once again and ready to take on tomorrow.


    Tomorrow’s route takes me back across the Puente Romántico and, knowing the Camino magic it can bring, I’ll plan to walk it slowly.
