  narrative: >-
    Today left my heart full. I took a chance and declined to reserve a place to
    stay and instead opted to try my chance at getting into a first come first
    serve albergue (pilgrims hostel).


    I had a particular hostel in mind. Betania,  named after Saint Martha de Betania, who showed hospitality to Jesus in the gospels. The albergue has only 20 beds and included a pilgrim’s dinner, in which we all ear together family style. 


    Committed to staying there, I got an early start. Too early it would seem. The doors opened at 1 and I arrived at 11. 


    I took a seat on the stoop assuming I’d have to wait around for 2 hours. 


    Luck was on my side. Not 2 minutes after sitting, Leah, one of the volunteers helping to run the albergue, unlocked the door and came outside to take the trash out. She confirmed the doors opened at 1 and that I’d have to wait. We got to talking. I told her my story, where I had come from and where I was going. She saw my bag - all 10 kilos of it - and said “you know what, why don’t you drop off your bag here. That way you can explore town for a bit without the extra weight.”  Leah clearly exemplified Saint Martha. I thanked her emphatically and went on my way. 


    Fast forward to later that afternoon.


    I returned to the albergue and was assigned a bed. 


    Not long after, one of the fellow pilgrims was playing guitar in the common room, specifically Imagine by John Lennon. I felt inspired to join in and sing with her. 


    One of the other volunteers heard me singing.   She let me know that after dinner, there would be a reflection in the 400 year old church attached to the albergue and asked whether I’d like to sing “the acoustics are amazing.” She just said the song would need to be religious. 


    I told her I used to sing Ave Maria all the time at family weddings. Perfect 


    It was a moment. Without my knowing it, the volunteer recorded it.
