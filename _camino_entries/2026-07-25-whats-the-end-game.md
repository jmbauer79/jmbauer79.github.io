    What’s the end game? 


    Knowing I would have a job waiting for me after wanderingthrough Europe for six weeks gave me confidence my journey wouldn’t ruin mefinancially and certainty it was happening. It felt real, and I becamecomfortable telling friends, family, and coworkers more broadly that, comeSeptember, I’d be flying across the globe to walk the Camino de Santiago, a pilgrimageof 500 miles by foot across Spain from the French border to the Cathedral ofSantiago de Compostela and beyond to the Atlantic coast. 


    Reactions varied widely. Many got it right away, and I’vehad multiple deep, encouraging conversations with those friends and colleagues.On the other hand, the responses from those who didn’t quite understand are theones that perhaps have been the most important prompts to stimulate my personalreflections and considerations on my motivations and plans: 


    “What made you wanna do that?”


    “Is this penance for something?” 


    “Do you think you can walk that far?


    “You’re gonna come back so fit!”


    “I wanna go to Spain too but not where you'll be. You need to go to Barcelona.”


    “One night in Madrid is not enough.”


    The questions and comments are fair. I recognize I’ve beenthinking about the Camino for years. I’ve reviewed countless firsthand accountsat this point. I’ve watched films. I’ve listened to over a hundred podcastepisodes, most of which include an interview with a pilgrim describing their motivationsand experiences. I’ve also spoken directly with pilgrims who’ve walked toSantiago. Collectively, these have further motivated me. I can’t expecteveryone to have the same understanding of the Camino and why it calls me.


    One more question I received came from a close friend. “Sowhat’s the endgame? Is this about being an influencer?” This one surprised meand hit me deeply because it came from a friend who knows me well.


    This friend loves Europe and history and has been excitedabout my journey. While my plan has been to walk the Camino alone, I’ve alsoexpressed an open invitation to friend and family that if anyone wants to meetme along the way, they are welcome to do so, while also making it clear that myitinerary is generally set. 


    The friend decided he will travel to Europe around the same andexpressed interest to overlap with me towards the end of my trek for a few daysor so. He asked about my plans and return flights, including the days afterSantiago. I shared that after Santiago, I’ll continue walking a few more daysto Fisterra, spend a couple days there, then take a bus down to Porto for acouple more days before flying back to California. 


    The response, paraphrased, came down to “Why Porto? Let’s doLisbon. It’s a lot more fun.”


    I explained I anticipated I’d be in a contemplative mood,and that spending time in a quieter town would likely be better, to allow me toreflect and write in my journal. I also already reserved what appeared to be abeautiful room with a lovely view of the Douro River.

    “Won’t you be writing in your journal for a month? What’sthe end game?”


    I was dumbstruck in the moment, surprised by the question. Igave a short response about personal reflection and growth, and theconversation moved on, but the question stuck with me. That I didn’t have readya satisfactory response ready to go stuck with me too.


    I reflected on the question over the next couple of days,which also prompted me to write this entry.


    As I've thought about, where my reflections have coalescedis that I don't know, and that's OK. I feel called to go on thiswalk. I'm motivated to maintain an open heart and mind while I'm on thejourney. While I do anticipate something will change within me, I really can'tsay what it will be, and I think that's OK.


    I can envision having epiphanies about parts of my life.Maybe I'll have clarity on things I'll realize I need to change or dodifferently. On the other hand, I may also gain confidence to keep doing whatI'm doing in other areas of my life. That is, in those cases, the epiphany maybe to remove doubts and increase certainty and confidence to keep going.The important thing, to me, is that I go in with an open heart and mind, andthat, if I hear God's voice, that I don't harden my heart.


    I've been working on journaling and capturingthese thoughts leading up to September 4 when I depart. I think it will beenlightening to be able to go back and read my thoughts in the months ofanticipation along with the journal entries on the Camino itself.
